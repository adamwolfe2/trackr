The CRM decision is the most consequential software purchase a revenue team makes. Everything else — outbound tools, enrichment, conversation intelligence, scheduling — connects to the CRM. If the CRM is wrong, the entire revenue stack is wrong.

This guide walks through how to make the CRM decision well, with specific guidance on the four major platforms and how to evaluate them against your actual requirements.

## Start with Requirements, Not Demos

The first mistake most CRM evaluations make is starting with vendor demos. Demos are designed to show what the tool does well, not whether it fits your specific situation. Start with requirements.

**Deal stages and pipeline structure**: How many pipelines do you need? Do deals move linearly or can they branch? Do you have multi-product lines with different stages?

**Team size and roles**: How many users will be in the system, and what roles will they have? SDRs, AEs, CSMs, and managers have different access and workflow needs.

**Data model requirements**: Do you need standard contacts and companies, or do you have a complex data model (e.g., parent-child account hierarchies, multi-location accounts, product-linked deals)?

**Integration requirements**: List the specific tools that need to connect to your CRM — outbound sequencer, marketing automation, billing, support platform, data warehouse. These integrations are non-negotiable; verify them before choosing a platform.

**Reporting requirements**: What reports does leadership expect from the CRM? Forecast accuracy, pipeline coverage, activity metrics, attribution reporting — different CRMs have very different native reporting capabilities.

Document these requirements before talking to any vendor. They become your scoring criteria.

## The Shortlist: When Each Platform Wins

### HubSpot — Wins for Most Teams

HubSpot is the right CRM for most companies in 2026. The user experience is far better than Salesforce, implementation is faster, and the native marketing and service hubs eliminate integration complexity. The AI features (contact summaries, email drafting, predictive lead scoring) are genuinely useful without requiring configuration.

**HubSpot wins when:**
- You want a single platform covering CRM, marketing automation, and support
- Your team is less technically sophisticated — HubSpot is genuinely learnable
- You're at Series A–C and want to scale without hiring a dedicated admin
- You're doing inbound and need tight marketing-to-sales attribution

**HubSpot pricing**: Free CRM (limited), Starter at $20/month, Pro at $90/user/month (where most meaningful features live), Enterprise at $150/user/month.

### Salesforce — Wins at Enterprise Scale

Salesforce is still the enterprise standard because of its customization depth, ecosystem, and reporting capability. With the right admin or implementation partner, Salesforce can do things HubSpot cannot — complex territory management, highly customized data models, Tableau CRM for advanced analytics. But the operational overhead is real.

**Salesforce wins when:**
- You have 50+ reps or complex territory/account structures
- Your revenue operations team has Salesforce-specific expertise
- You need deep customization beyond what HubSpot's data model supports
- Your board or CFO expects Salesforce forecasting

**Salesforce pricing**: Starter at $25/user/month, Pro Suite at $100/user/month, Enterprise at $165/user/month. Expect to add 20–40% for implementation, admin, and apps.

### Attio — Wins for Relationship-Driven Teams

Attio is the modern CRM built for how startups actually work. Flexible data model, clean interface, and native AI features that feel genuinely product-native rather than bolted on. The relationship graph — seeing connections between people, companies, and deals — is uniquely powerful for founder-led or partnership-heavy sales motions.

**Attio wins when:**
- You're doing founder-led or relationship-driven sales
- Your CRM is as much about managing a network as tracking a pipeline
- You want flexibility without Salesforce's complexity
- Your team is technical and wants a CRM that doesn't feel dated

**Attio pricing**: Free (limited), Plus at $34/user/month, Pro at $74/user/month.

### Pipedrive — Wins for Simple Pipeline Management

Pipedrive is the most opinionated of the four — it does pipeline management very well and does not try to be everything else. For companies that want a simple, visual sales pipeline without the complexity of HubSpot's full platform, Pipedrive is an efficient choice.

**Pipedrive wins when:**
- You want simple, visual pipeline management without a learning curve
- Your team does not need marketing automation or support native in the same platform
- You want low-cost per seat for a sales-focused team
- You're moving fast and need something running in days, not weeks

**Pipedrive pricing**: Essential at $14/user/month, Advanced at $29/user/month, Professional at $59/user/month.

## How to Run a 2-Week Pilot Effectively

A CRM pilot needs real data and real workflows to produce useful signal. Structure it this way:

**Week 1 — Data import and workflow setup**: Import at least 200 real contacts and 20 real deals. Build the pipeline stages you actually use. Connect the integrations that are non-negotiable.

**Week 2 — Live selling**: Have the sales team use the CRM for all actual selling activity — logging calls, updating deals, sequencing emails. The test is whether people naturally use it or work around it.

At the end of week two, hold a debrief with the team. Specifically ask: what felt slow or clunky? What did you have to work around? What worked better than expected?

## 7 Questions to Ask in the Vendor Demo

- How does the data model handle [your most complex account structure]?
- Walk me through what happens when a deal is closed-won — what data is captured and where?
- How do we connect this to [your primary outbound tool or marketing platform]?
- What does the admin experience look like — who manages users, permissions, and custom fields?
- How do we get our data out if we decide to switch?
- What does the onboarding process look like and what does it cost?
- What is the most common reason companies in our situation leave your platform?

## Pricing Comparison at 10, 50, and 200 Users

At 10 users:
- HubSpot Pro: $900/month
- Salesforce Professional: $1,000/month
- Attio Pro: $740/month
- Pipedrive Professional: $590/month

At 50 users:
- HubSpot Pro: $4,500/month
- Salesforce Professional: $5,000/month (before implementation overhead)
- Attio Pro: $3,700/month
- Pipedrive Professional: $2,950/month

At 200 users, implementation costs, admin overhead, and required add-ons begin to diverge significantly. Salesforce's total cost of ownership at 200+ users is typically 40–60% higher than HubSpot when you account for admin, implementation partner costs, and required apps.

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Pricing that requires three conversations to understand
- Integration partnerships that are actually Zapier connections in disguise
- Demo data that doesn't represent realistic complexity
- Renewal terms with auto-escalation clauses above 10%
- No credible customers in your industry or at your stage
