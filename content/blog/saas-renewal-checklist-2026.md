The worst SaaS decision is not a bad initial purchase — it's a thoughtless renewal. A bad initial purchase you can cancel after 30 days. An unconsidered renewal locks you in for another year at potentially the wrong price, with a tool that may no longer be the best option in its category.

The 60-day window before a renewal date is your leverage window. Use it to ask these 12 questions before you sign anything.

## The 60-Day Renewal Window

Most SaaS contracts auto-renew unless you cancel within 30, 45, or 60 days of the renewal date. That notice period is buried in the contract — which is exactly where vendors want it.

Set a calendar reminder 60 days before every renewal. That gives you time to run through these 12 questions, gather utilization data, evaluate alternatives, and negotiate — all before you've lost the leverage of having a credible walk-away option.

## The 12 Questions

### 1. What's the Actual Utilization Rate?

This is the first question because it is the most important. Pull the admin dashboard and look at active users, feature usage, and login frequency over the last 90 days.

- What percentage of licensed seats are active monthly?
- What percentage of the feature set is the team using?
- Has usage increased, decreased, or flatlined since you originally purchased?

A tool with 40% of seats inactive is a negotiating position. A tool with 100% utilization and a waitlist for new seats is a tool you keep and potentially expand.

### 2. Has the Landscape Changed Since We Last Evaluated?

AI has reshuffled most software categories in the last 18–24 months. A tool that was clearly best-in-class when you bought it may now have direct competitors offering better functionality at lower cost.

Ask specifically: have any well-funded alternatives launched or significantly improved since your last evaluation?

### 3. What Are We Paying vs. Market Rate?

Vendors offer introductory pricing that escalates at renewal. List prices change. Your contract may be significantly above or below current market rate — and you probably don't know which without checking.

Look up current published pricing for this tool and its top two competitors. Calculate your per-seat or per-unit cost and compare.

### 4. Are There New Competitors Worth a Quick Evaluation?

You don't need to run a full vendor evaluation to answer this question. Spend 20 minutes understanding what has emerged. If something looks meaningfully better, add it to a short pilot before the renewal date.

### 5. What Features Have Been Released?

Vendors release features all the time that many customers never notice. Before renewal, review the vendor's changelog or release notes for the last 12 months.

You may discover features that eliminate a complaint your team had about the tool. You may also discover that features you were promised have still not shipped.

### 6. Is There Pricing Leverage at Renewal?

Pricing leverage exists when:
- You have a credible alternative you are willing to switch to
- You are considering a seat reduction
- The vendor knows you are an active evaluator, not a passive renewer
- The contract is above market rate (see Question 3)

Every renewal is a negotiation. The vendor wants to keep your business. Use that.

### 7. Is Multi-Year Worth the Discount?

Vendors typically offer 15–25% discounts for 2-year commitments and 20–30% for 3-year. The math is simple: calculate the breakeven point (how many months of discount savings offset the commitment risk) and compare it to your confidence that you'll still want this tool.

Do not sign multi-year without a clear picture of your growth trajectory and the tool's roadmap.

### 8. What Does the Vendor's Roadmap Look Like?

Ask the vendor for a product roadmap preview as part of renewal conversations. This serves two purposes: you get useful information about where the product is going, and you signal that you are an engaged customer who evaluates before renewing.

Red flags: vague roadmaps, promises without timelines, features that have been "coming soon" for more than two quarters.

### 9. Are There Integration Gaps Hurting Workflows?

After a year of use, integration limitations are well-understood. The question is whether those limitations are getting better or staying static.

Does the tool sync reliably with your CRM? Does it export data in the format your reporting layer needs? Unresolved integration gaps often drive quiet disengagement — people stop using a tool not because it's bad but because it doesn't connect to where their work actually lives.

### 10. What's the Switching Cost If We Leave?

Be honest about this. Switching costs include:
- Time to migrate data out of the current tool and into the alternative
- Retraining time for the team
- Rebuilding integrations and automations
- The risk of productivity loss during the transition

High switching costs are not a reason to stay in a bad tool forever — but they should be included in any honest comparison. A tool that is 20% better but requires three months of migration may not be worth switching right now.

### 11. What Do Team Members Actually Say About It?

Utilization data tells you what people do. Qualitative feedback tells you why. Before renewal, ask the primary users of the tool:

- What's working well?
- What's frustrating?
- If we switched to [alternative], would that bother you?
- What would make this tool significantly more valuable to you?

Team sentiment matters. A tool your team hates but uses at 80% utilization is different from a tool your team loves and uses at 80% utilization.

## How to Negotiate

Armed with answers to the 12 questions, the negotiation conversation is simple. Contact your account manager or renewal contact 45 days before renewal and say:

"We're in our renewal window and evaluating our options. We've been happy with [Tool], but we've also noticed [specific alternatives] have improved significantly. We'd like to continue, but we'd need to see [specific ask — 20% discount / seat reduction / added features] to make this an easy decision."

Most vendors will work with you. The ones that won't are showing you something about the relationship.

## When to Switch vs. Renew

Switch if:
- Utilization is below 40% with no clear path to improvement
- A meaningfully better alternative is available without excessive switching cost
- The vendor is unresponsive, unstable, or not investing in the product
- The price is more than 30% above market rate and the vendor won't move

Renew if:
- Utilization is high and the team values the tool
- Switching cost exceeds the benefit of the alternative
- The vendor has demonstrated investment in the product and responsiveness to feedback
- The price is competitive or improved at renewal

The discipline is running through this process every time, not just when something is obviously broken. Most SaaS waste comes from quiet auto-renewals, not bad initial decisions.
