## The Global Hiring Problem

Hiring internationally is genuinely hard without the right infrastructure. Employment law, payroll compliance, benefits, equity, and termination rules vary enormously by country. Getting it wrong isn't just expensive — it creates legal liability.

Deel and Remote are the two leading platforms for solving this problem. Both offer Employer of Record (EOR) services, global payroll, contractor management, and compliance tools. The differences are real but come down to specific needs.

## What Both Platforms Do

**Employer of Record (EOR):** Both platforms legally employ your international workers on your behalf. The EOR handles local employment contracts, tax withholding, benefits enrollment, and compliance — you manage the day-to-day work. EOR is the right structure when you want full-time employees abroad without establishing a local entity.

**Contractor Management:** Both platforms handle contractor agreements, compliance classification, and payment to international contractors in local currencies. This is simpler than EOR and appropriate for independent contractors.

**Global Payroll:** For companies with established entities in multiple countries, both platforms can run payroll — calculating taxes, handling deductions, and processing payments in local currencies.

## Country Coverage

**Deel** claims coverage in 150+ countries, including some markets where other platforms don't operate — specific African countries, less common Southeast Asian markets, and several Eastern European jurisdictions.

**Remote** claims coverage in 170+ countries but with varying service levels across that footprint. Remote's owned-entity model (rather than partner-based EOR in some markets) is a meaningful differentiator for compliance quality.

**Key distinction:** Remote prioritizes building owned legal entities in countries rather than using local partners. This provides more consistent compliance quality but means their genuine owned-entity coverage is smaller than the headline number. Deel uses a hybrid of owned entities and local partners.

For most common markets (US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, Netherlands, Australia, Singapore), both platforms offer strong service. The differences emerge in less common markets.

**Verdict: Verify both on your specific country list** — headlines are less useful than checking the specific countries you need.

## Pricing (2026)

**Deel:**
- EOR: $599/employee/month
- Contractors: $49/contractor/month (with their free tier for basic contractor management)
- Global payroll: Starting at $29/employee/month for existing entity payroll

**Remote:**
- EOR: $599/employee/month (on annual plan) or $699 monthly
- Contractors: $29/contractor/month
- Global payroll: Starting at $50/employee/month

At the EOR level, pricing is essentially identical. Remote is cheaper for contractors; Deel is cheaper for global payroll. Both negotiate for larger teams.

## Compliance and Legal Quality

Remote's owned-entity model gives them stronger compliance control in covered markets. When Remote employs your worker, they're using their own local subsidiary — not a third-party partner who may have different standards.

Deel's partner model in some markets introduces a variable: the compliance quality depends on the local partner. Deel mitigates this through vetting and contractual standards, but the control is less direct.

For companies where compliance risk is a primary concern, Remote's owned-entity approach is more conservative and often preferred by legal teams.

**Verdict: Remote** on compliance conservatism.

## Product and Experience

**Deel** has historically had a stronger product. The Deel dashboard is well-designed, the contractor management flow is smooth, and equity management integrations are available. Deel's Slack integration for HR workflows is useful. The overall product feel is more polished.

**Remote** has closed the product gap significantly. The platform is clean, the onboarding flow for new employees is good, and the self-service benefits enrollment in many markets is a genuine improvement. Remote's employee experience interface is competitive.

**Verdict: Roughly equal** — both have strong products, Deel historically with a slight edge on polish.

## HRIS and Integration Features

Deel has expanded into HRIS territory, offering payroll for US domestic employees, equity management, and a full HR platform beyond just international hiring. For startups wanting a single HR platform that handles both US and international, Deel's breadth is an advantage.

Remote is more focused on the international hiring core use case, with integrations to external HRIS platforms (BambooHR, Workday, etc.) rather than trying to replace them.

**Verdict: Deel** for all-in-one HR; Remote for best-of-breed international hiring integrated with your existing HR stack.

## Customer Support

Both platforms have expanded support teams as they've scaled. Both offer dedicated account management at higher volume tiers. User reviews on G2 show:

- Deel: Generally strong reviews on product, some complaints about support responsiveness for complex compliance questions
- Remote: Strong reviews on compliance support quality, some complaints about slower product iteration

**Verdict: Remote** is preferred for compliance-intensive support; Deel for product experience.

## Which to Choose

**Choose Deel if:**
- You want a single platform for US payroll, international EOR, and contractor management
- Equity management and HRIS features matter
- You value product polish and a more complete platform
- You're hiring in many markets simultaneously and need broad coverage

**Choose Remote if:**
- Compliance rigor is your primary concern and you want owned-entity EOR
- You have an existing HRIS and want best-of-breed international hiring that integrates with it
- Contractor management at a lower per-contractor cost matters
- You're in a market where Remote's owned-entity model offers a clear advantage
